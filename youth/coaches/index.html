Our shared commitment

A consistent foundation. Across every team.

Coaches bring their own personalities and styles. But the skills we teach, and the habits we reinforce, should not depend on which team a child joins.

  1. 01 · Every practice

    Teach the fundamentals

    A written plan every practice. A few moves, taught well, lots of reps. Kids leave tired, a little better, and excited to come back.

  2. 02 · Every wrestler

    Every kid matters

    The beginner gets the same attention as the tournament winner. Partners and live goes are matched by size and experience.

  3. 03 · Every skill

    Master it before moving up

    A skill is learned when the wrestler can demonstrate it, not when the coach has shown it. Levels are skills, not years.

  4. 04 · Every match

    Matches are for experience

    In the corner: calm, short, positive. The first words after a match are positive. Fixing happens at the next practice.

The Northampton habit

Finish. Get up. Stop the first move.

Every level, every practice. On our feet we finish shots and go behind, never around the waist. On bottom we move on the whistle and get to our feet. On top we stop the first move and don't let them up.

Stance → shot → finish → feet.

What success looks like

By the start of 7th grade

A wrestler finishing Orange & Black should be able to demonstrate:

On our feet
Hand fight to a tie and shoot from it. Finish head outside, head inside, low and high singles on a resisting partner without leaving the leg. Counter the singles and score from a front headlock.
Bottom
Hold a base, get to their feet with the stand-up series and sit to stand, switch and re-switch, fight the chop, and clear legs.
Top
Break down and Turk, finish the crossface and near cradles and the half, run a bar, and ride legs.
Attitude
Wrestle hard all six minutes, win the 3rd period, never get a stall call, and fight off their back.

These fundamentals carry straight into the junior high room.

Development first, not a medal count. Growth over results. Improvement, discipline and effort are the goal; winning is a byproduct. Matches are for experience, and every kid who wants matches gets them. A coach is measured by whether kids leave loving the sport and trusting their coaches.

The development process

Four steps to an effective season

  1. 1

    Before the season

    Select your level: Novice or Orange & Black. Read its attitude, coaching habits, and the coaching standards.

  2. 2

    Every week

    Choose 1–2 skills from your level's list. Build each practice around them with the practice builder.

  3. 3

    At practice

    Teach the cues, drill it (Orange & Black drills 2&2), put it in a live situation, then check: can the wrestler demonstrate it?

  4. 4

    After practice

    Coach's notes: who owns today's skill, and who needs it re-taught next practice.

Our mission

To help student-athletes develop the timeless qualities and skills they need to become successful and happy adults, to help them reach their potential in wrestling, and to add value to their lives.

Better people first. Better wrestlers along the way.

The position ladder

Teach

  • Back is worst.
  • Belly beats back.
  • Referee's position beats belly.
  • Tripod or sit beats referee's position.
  • Feet beat everything.

Non-negotiable coaching habits

  • A few moves, taught well, with lots of reps.
  • Games whenever possible; conditioning built into games.
  • Every kid gets the same attention, beginners most of all.
  • Partners and live goes matched by size and experience.
  • Praise effort and attitude more than results.

Teaching distinction

No 2&2 at Novice. Use partner drills, short reps and games, with one or two new moves at a time.

Live situations

  • Live once in on a leg: head outside, head inside, high single (good or bad position).
  • On knees: one shoots, the other counters on the whistle.
  • On belly: get to your knees, get to a sit, get to your feet.
  • On knees: maintain position.
  • Half nelson, bottom on knees or belly: get out or don't get out.
  • Near cradle, crossface, crossface cradle.
  • Quick starts (5–30 seconds).

Experienced wrestlers on Northampton Orange and Northampton Black, varsity and JV.

Orange & Black

Keep drilling everything in Novice. Orange and Black adds the following.

O&B

Build on Novice. Re-teach any Novice skill a wrestler can't demonstrate before adding new ones. Then add finishes, counters, hand fighting, chains and legs.

Attitude · the Northampton way

  • We never give up. Hustle back to the center.
  • We don't go out of bounds; keep your back to the center and circle in.
  • We hustle/run back to the center of the mat when we go out of bounds.
  • We wrestle hard on the edge of the mat.
  • We don't get outscored or out-fought in the 3rd period.
  • We do not have a “crybaby face” at any time during or after the bout.
  • We do not get any stall calls against us.
  • We do not display unsportsmanlike conduct (whether called or not).
  • We do not get pinned. We fight off our back.
  • We never use injury time to help us win. That is unethical and unacceptable.

Bottom13 skills

Rule one: don't do something dumb. Don't put yourself on your back or belly. Don't give up your wrist (no hand control until you're fighting to your feet). Don't let legs in. Whichever way your opponent wants to take you, go the opposite way.

Base

Teach

Exit check: not just activity

Can they actually do it?

At the end, ask 3–5 wrestlers to demonstrate today’s skill. If most can’t do it under light pressure, keep it as the priority next practice.

The practice plan, section by section

  1. Announcements Keep it short. Skin check and attendance as kids line up.
  2. Warm-up Movement and games. No lifting at the youth level.
  3. Drill (review principles) 3–5 moves already taught, with their key cues. Orange & Black drills 2&2.
  4. New Technique 1–2 moves, marked (new). Teach the cues, then lots of reps.
  5. Contest situation Live situations matched by size and experience. Short goes.
  6. Conditioning A conditioning game. Takes the place of CrossFit at the youth level.
  7. Announcements One quick reminder if needed.
  8. Team Culture Spotlight or Observations (“What did you see someone do well?”). Every kid shakes every coach's hand on the way out.
  9. My notes Who owns today's move? Who needs it re-taught next practice?
