Our shared standard

One program. One way of teaching.

Coaches bring their own personalities. What we teach, and how we treat kids, doesn’t depend on which team a child joins.

  1. 01 · Every practice

    Teach the fundamentals

    A written plan every practice. A few moves, taught well, lots of reps. Kids leave tired, a little better, and excited to come back.

  2. 02 · Every wrestler

    Every kid matters

    The beginner gets the same attention as the tournament winner. Partners and live goes are matched by size and experience.

  3. 03 · Every skill

    Master it before moving up

    A skill is learned when the wrestler can demonstrate it, not when the coach has shown it. Levels are skills, not years.

  4. 04 · Every match

    Matches are for experience

    In the corner: calm, short, positive. The first words after a match are positive. Fixing happens at the next practice.

Teach the Northampton way. Every group is organized the same way, Attitude, On Our Feet, Bottom, Top, in the same words the high school room uses.

Our mission

To help student-athletes develop the timeless qualities and skills they need to become successful and happy adults, to help them reach their potential in wrestling, and to add value to their lives.

Better people first. Better wrestlers along the way.

How to use this playbook

Four steps to a good season

  1. 1

    Before the season

    Pick your group. Read its attitude, coaching habits, and the coaching standards.

  2. 2

    Every week

    Choose 1–2 skills from your group's list. Build each practice around them with the practice builder.

  3. 3

    At practice

    Teach the cues, drill it (Orange & Black drills 2&2), put it in a live situation, then check: can the wrestler demonstrate it?

  4. 4

    After practice

    Coach's notes: who owns today's skill, and who needs it re-taught next practice.

What to teach, and how

Pick your group

First- and second-year wrestlers, mostly K–2.

Novice

Few moves, taught well, lots of reps.

Keep it fun. Fun is a requirement, not a reward. The more fun they have, the more they improve. Use games, short reps, and lots of praise for effort.

Build a Novice practice

Coaching habits

  • A few moves, taught well, with lots of reps.
  • Games whenever possible; conditioning built into games.
  • Every kid gets the same attention, beginners most of all.
  • Partners and live goes matched by size and experience.
  • Praise effort and attitude more than results.

Attitude

  • We never give up.
  • We wrestle hard at all times.
  • We always hustle.
  • We respect teammates, coaches, opponents, and officials.

On Our Feet13 skills

On every shot: don't stay on your knees (drive in or tripod up). Go behind, not around the waist. Don't just hold a leg; try to score.

Position

Stance

Coach it this way

  1. Teach
    • Staggered and square.
    • Low, balanced, ready.
    • Always able to touch the mat with your hand.
  2. Drill

    Stance in, stance out on the coach's call: staggered, square, touch the mat.

  3. Live

    Ankle touch for points.

Check: wrestler demonstratesHolds both stances in motion and can touch the mat with either hand at any time without standing up.

The position ladder

Coach it this way

  1. Teach
    • Back is worst.
    • Belly beats back.
    • Referee's position beats belly.
    • Tripod or sit beats referee's position.
    • Feet beat everything.
    • Always get to something better.
  2. Drill

    Coach calls a position (back, belly, knees, sit); wrestlers get to the next better one.

  3. Live

    On belly: get to your knees, get to a sit, get to your feet.

Check: wrestler demonstratesNames the ladder in order, and when broken down to the belly, works back to the knees.

Live situations

  • Live once in on a leg: head outside, head inside, high single (good or bad position).
  • On knees: one shoots, the other counters on the whistle.
  • On belly: get to your knees, get to a sit, get to your feet.
  • On knees: maintain position.
  • Half nelson, bottom on knees or belly: get out or don't get out.
  • Near cradle, crossface, crossface cradle.
  • Quick starts (5–30 seconds).

Experienced wrestlers on Northampton Orange and Northampton Black, varsity and JV.

Orange & Black

Keep drilling everything in Novice. Orange and Black adds the following.

Build on Novice. Re-teach any Novice skill a wrestler can't demonstrate before adding new ones. Then add finishes, counters, hand fighting, chains and legs.

Build a Orange & Black practice

Coaching habits

  • Keep drilling Novice skills every week.
  • Chain moves: connect one move to the next instead of teaching moves alone.
  • Live situations every practice, matched by size and experience.
  • Measure effort and attitude, not the scoreboard.
  • Same language as the high school room.

Attitude

  • We never give up. Hustle back to the center.
  • We don't go out of bounds; keep your back to the center and circle in.
  • We hustle/run back to the center of the mat when we go out of bounds.
  • We wrestle hard on the edge of the mat.
  • We don't get outscored or out-fought in the 3rd period.
  • We do not have a “crybaby face” at any time during or after the bout.
  • We do not get any stall calls against us.
  • We do not display unsportsmanlike conduct (whether called or not).
  • We do not get pinned. We fight off our back.
  • We never use injury time to help us win. That is unethical and unacceptable.

Position and hand fighting

Stalking

Coach it this way

  1. Teach
    • Close the gap, make contact.
    • Either have space with motion and fakes, or contact your opponent and move them backwards.
  2. Drill

    Shadow wrestle with fakes and level changes, then hand fight and move your partner back.

  3. Live

    Live hand fight (shoulder contact or hands to the mat scores a point).

Check: wrestler demonstratesIn a live hand fight, closes the gap and moves a partner backwards instead of standing in place.

Bottom13 skills

Rule one: don't do something dumb. Don't put yourself on your back or belly. Don't give up your wrist (no hand control until you're fighting to your feet). Don't let legs in. Whichever way your opponent wants to take you, go the opposite way.

Base

Coach it this way

  1. Teach
    • Build it and hold it.
    • Elbow tight to the side; don't let your opponent under the arm.
    • Foot out on the belly side, small sit, back pressure, chest up.
    • Don't look down for hands.
  2. Drill

    Top breaks down at half speed; bottom holds base.

  3. Live

    On knees: maintain position.

Check: wrestler demonstratesHolds base for 10 seconds while the top wrestler tries to break them down.

Live situations

Everything in Novice, plus:

Neutral

  • Foot touch; foot touch and re-shot.
  • Front headlock. Crackdown. Low single with head through.
  • Head inside single with a hook; with hands locked.
  • Tie-ups: one wrestler has a collar tie, a 2-on-1 or an underhook.
  • Live hand fight (shoulder contact or hands to the mat scores a point).
  • One wrestler on offense, one on defense, with or without a stall warning.

Top and bottom

  • Arm bar, bottom on belly or knees. Bar and wrist. Cross wrist. Arm fed through.
  • One leg in; two legs in (on knees or on belly). Top has an ankle.
  • One on one.
  • Sitting, with and without hand control.
  • On a hip with a tight waist.
  • Standing, with and without hand control.

Match situations

  • Quick starts with a score given (down 1, down 2) and stalling.
  • Overtime ride-out.

Practice builder

Same practice plan the high school room uses, adjusted for youth. Every practice has a written plan. Kids should leave tired, a little better, and excited to come back. Pick your group, the moves to review, and the new technique. The plan fills in the cues, the contest situation, and what to check.

Review moves (3–5)

The practice plan, section by section

  1. AnnouncementsKeep it short. Skin check and attendance as kids line up.
  2. Warm-upMovement and games. No lifting at the youth level.
  3. Drill (review principles)3–5 moves already taught, with their key cues. Orange & Black drills 2&2.
  4. New Technique1–2 moves, marked (new). Teach the cues, then lots of reps.
  5. Contest situationLive situations matched by size and experience. Short goes.
  6. ConditioningA conditioning game. Takes the place of CrossFit at the youth level.
  7. AnnouncementsOne quick reminder if needed.
  8. Team CultureSpotlight or Observations (“What did you see someone do well?”). Every kid shakes every coach's hand on the way out.
  9. My notesWho owns today's move? Who needs it re-taught next practice?

  • Head inside single
    • Go behind: don't wrap the lower leg, post the arm through, extend the leg, put it on the table, tackle the other leg.
    • Knee under the ankle and come up.
    • Opponent whizzers: head behind the leg, or lock hands above the knee.
    • Drive up and knee pick.
    • Hook and dive through to a sit, pull to the side, Turk.
    • Slide the head across to the double.

  • vs. head outside single
    • Foot touch run down.

Coaching standards

We are transformational coaches. Kids learn what a good adult looks like by watching us.

  1. Safety first, always.
  2. Make it fun.
  3. Every kid matters.
  4. Growth over results.
  5. Correct in a way that lifts kids up.
  6. No yelling, profanity, or sarcasm.
  7. Never alone with a child. No private messages.
  8. No weight cutting at the youth level.
  9. Respect officials and opponents.
  10. Parents are partners. Use the chain of communication.
  11. Teach the Northampton way.
  12. When in doubt, ask Coach Provini.
